07 · Accent matrix

Five chromatic accents plus two neutrals. The chromatic five all produce a mid-dark bar, so bar text stays white and every shadow engraves. Silver produces a light bar and Black a very dark one, and both flip things the other five never touch — which is why the bar fills had to become tokens rather than literals.

08 · Implementation notes

Most of Legacy is paint, not structure. Every chrome band already has the height iOS 6 used — status bar 20, nav bar 44, tab bar 50 against iOS 6's 49. The split view, the virtualised row heights, the rail pitch and the mini player all stay exactly as they are. That is why this can ship as a theme rather than a fork.
1 · state.dark has to become tri-state. The skin stores a boolean. Legacy makes it theme: 'light' | 'dark' | 'legacy', and the per-surface overrides (miniTheme, smallTheme, fullTheme) inherit the same enum. Write a migration that reads the old boolean, and keep writing it for one release so a downgrade does not land users on an unknown value.
2 · Settings import validation will reject the new value unless it is updated in the same commit. This is the documented trap that already bit the Apps tab: settings.js keeps its own copy of several ui.js enums. Adding a theme without touching it means every exported preferences file containing legacy fails to import, silently.
3 · Do not let Legacy grow any chrome band. The A–Z rail needs a viewport about 893px tall to show all 27 entries, and it already loses letters on a 1512 × 861 MacBook. Anything Legacy adds vertically — a taller nav bar, a thicker toolbar, a chunkier tab bar — comes straight out of the rail and makes EC-002 worse. Decoration only.
4 · tools/check-contrast.py covers ten scenarios: two themes × five accents. Legacy makes that fifteen, and it is the theme most likely to fail — emboss and engrave shadows let a design look readable while the underlying pair does not pass. Add the third theme to the tool before the first Legacy commit, not after, and expect the grey-on-grey secondary text (--text2 on --group-bg) to be the first casualty.
5 · Two chrome families, on purpose. iOS 6 itself ran a slate nav bar in Settings and a silver one in Music. Legacy keeps both: slate for the navigation bar, silver for the library toolbar and mini player. If you would rather have one, the slate is the more recognisable of the two — say so and I will collapse it.
6 · Accent tinting uses color-mix(). All five accent schemes drive the nav gradient, the selected row and the active tab glow from one seed colour. If the skin still has to run on a browser without color-mix, the fallback is five hand-written gradient sets — more code, no behaviour change.
7 · The iframe is the only screen in the skin that is not the skin. Two routes, and they are not equivalent. Style injection — the embedded page is same-origin, so iframe.contentDocument is reachable and a Legacy stylesheet can be dropped into it. Cheap, no new API surface, and purely cosmetic: it does not fix EC-008 or EC-030, because the embedded page keeps its own tab strip and its own history. Native rendering — replace the iframe with Echo Classic markup, as drawn in section 03. That fixes both defects outright and gives the split view for free. [Unverified] I have not established which LMS settings pages are reachable as structured data versus HTML forms only; that has to be checked before anyone commits to it. My recommendation is native for Manage Plugins, injection for the long tail.
8 · Manage Plugins is the one page where native rendering is clearly worth it. The audit measured that page at 340,527 bytes of embedded HTML for what is, in substance, about thirty records with five fields each. It is also the page users hit most often. [Unverified] whether LMS exposes the extensions registry over JSON-RPC — ExtensionsManager.pm, extensions.prefs and state.prefs hold the data, but the wire surface has not been confirmed.
9 · If Echo Classic draws its own plugin manager, it inherits the filtering rules that have already bitten this project twice. Plugins.pm::_addInfo prunes entries from a repository section, and the plugin page's category filter runs browser-side comparing s.category == id — which is why a missing <category> made Echo Classic vanish from its own listing, and why <enforce> made it perpetually absent from Active Plugins. A native grid must reproduce that logic exactly or it will show a plugin the server will not install.
10 · The status bar tint in this mockup is derived, not chosen. Per the iOS 6 review you linked, Apple tinted the status bar from a colour averaged out of the navigation bar's bottom row of pixels rather than keeping it black. Legacy does the same thing with color-mix() against the accent seed, so all five accent schemes get a status bar that belongs to their nav bar. Change the accent above and watch the top strip follow.
11 · The neutral accents separate chrome colour from interactive tint, and they have to. The other five drive both from one seed. A silver or black seed used as the link colour would put grey-on-white or near-black-on-white links next to near-black body text — technically passing contrast while telling the user nothing. So Silver tints links #4d5967 and Black #343a42, and both lean on the selected-row gradient and the chevron to carry affordance instead of hue. [Inference] That is a reasoned trade-off, not a measured one — I have not user-tested whether the remaining cue is enough, and it is the one thing in this theme I would put in front of a real person before shipping.
12 · The contrast matrix is now 21 scenarios, not 15. tools/check-contrast.py currently runs two themes × five accents. Legacy took that to fifteen; Silver and Black take it to twenty-one. Silver is the highest-risk cell in the whole grid — it is the only combination with dark text on a light bar, so every pair the tool was written to check for white-on-slate is inverted, and the emboss shadow will make it look fine at a glance whether or not it passes.
